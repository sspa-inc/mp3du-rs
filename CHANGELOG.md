# Changelog

## 0.1.9 (2026-11-26)

Changes since 0.1.8. This is a speed release for transient models on Waterloo velocity fields: the fit of each cell is built once, by a faster solve that is also more accurate in some cells, and is reused in later time steps. A steady run, or the first time step of a transient one, gains only from the faster first fit of each cell.
- **Water-level-map (SSP&A) runs** do not use the changed code, so they are unchanged. They were not re-run: the change is confined to the Waterloo fit.
- **Waterloo runs change slightly:**
  - **In most cells, only by round-off.** Where the fit's condition number is below about 1e4, the fitted velocities agree with 0.1.8's to round-off.
  - **In some moderately ill-conditioned cells, by up to about 4e-5 (relative) in velocity.** The fit is now solved by QR with column pivoting, which is more accurate there than 0.1.8's SVD solve. Checked against exact arithmetic on 24 cells of a Voronoi grid, QR's error was at most 6e-8, while the old solve's reached 3e-5. In the transient example below, 20 of 446 cells changed by more than 1e-9.
  - **Nearly singular cells,** such as long, thin cells or cells fitted far from their centroid, still use the SVD, as before.
  - **Particles near a capture face** can move visibly.
- **In a transient two-well example** (446 Voronoi cells, 600 particles, a fit every 15 days):
  - no particle changed fate;
  - positions moved by up to 6.6 m (99th percentile 0.43 m), and capture times by up to 4.3 days. A last-digit change of the flows moves 0.1.8's results by up to 3.1 m and 4.3 days.
- **In the User Guide's Examples 1a, 2, 3, 6 and 7:** no particle changed fate. End points moved by about as much as last-digit changes of the flows move them; the most was 0.58 ft, for particle 1 of Example 1a.
- **Dispersion runs on Waterloo fields** can follow different random paths once a round-off difference shifts a step. This has not been tested in MP3DU-RS. When the same change was made in mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9, its Example 4A gave the same plume statistics (mean x 300.45 against 300.50, standard error 0.5).

### Changed
- **The Waterloo velocity fit is now built once per cell and reused.**
  - **How:** the least-squares fit of a cell's velocity series is linear in the cell's flows, and its matrix depends only on the cell's shape. MP3DU-RS now keeps one small matrix per cell, so fitting a cell to new flows in a later time step is one matrix–vector product.
  - **Speed:**
    - building a cell's fit takes about 0.6 ms instead of about 1.3 ms (QR; the SVD remains for nearly singular cells);
    - a later refit of a cell takes about 0.5–0.8 µs instead of about 1.2 ms;
    - on a grid of 102,400 cells, fitting and evaluating every cell again in a second time step took 0.21 s instead of 136 s;
    - the whole transient two-well script ran in 3.2 s instead of 5.3 s (best of 8 runs each, MODFLOW 6 included: a saving of about 2 s).
  - **Who gains:** the first fit of a cell takes about half as long as before; scripts that refit the same grid in many time steps gain most.
  - **To benefit in a transient script,** create one `WaterlooConfig` and pass the same object to `fit_waterloo` at every time step. A matrix is reused only for a cell whose geometry is the same, bit for bit. A new `WaterlooConfig` per call still works, but reuses nothing; so does alternating one config between two grids.
  - **Memory:**
    - a kept `WaterlooConfig` holds about 4 KB for each cell that particles have visited (4.5 KB for a cell with 6 faces);
    - on 102,400 visited cells the process peak rose from 274 MB to 687 MB;
    - a config that is not kept holds nothing.
  - **New methods:** `WaterlooConfig.cache_info()` reports what is held, and `clear_cache()` releases it. Call `clear_cache()` after a run that visits very many cells. Cells are fitted when a particle first reaches them, so `cache_info()` shows counts only after `run_simulation`.
- **Agreement with mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9.** The mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 update of 11/26/2026 uses the same method: a least-squares fit, built once per cell and solved by QR with column pivoting.
  - **In nearly singular cells the codes differ.** MP3DU-RS falls back to the SVD there; 2.1.9 keeps pivots down to round-off.
  - **In the cells of the User Guide's Examples 3 and 6,** the fitted velocities of the two codes agree to about 1e-13.
  - **Travel times can still differ by more than the fits do.** The adaptive step control reacts to round-off at a few points, so a single particle's time can change by up to about 0.2% (Example 3). Changing the flows only in their last digits moves MP3DU-RS's own results as much. Averaged over Example 3's particles, the two codes differ by about 0.01–0.03%.
- **Rust API:** `WaterlooField::with_cache`, `FitOperatorCache` and `CacheInfo`.

### Known issues
As described for 0.1.8, and:
- **In flow that turns sharply,** for example under recharge, long steps bias GSDE dispersion. In the User Guide's Example 7 with dispersion (αL 10, αTH 1, αTV 0.1), steps with no limit made the mean capture time about 5% later than steps of 10 days, in both MP3DU-RS and mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9. Limit `max_dt` until the result stops changing. A revised dispersion scheme is in development.
- **Capture in a cell entered through a side face** (an internal sink such as a constant-head cell) is recorded at the end of the advective step that entered the cell, not at the face. The capture time can therefore be late by up to one step: by about 4 days of 1,339 for one particle of the User Guide's Example 1a (6 days of 1,341 in 0.1.8).

## 0.1.8 (2026-11-25)

Changes since 0.1.7. Results change with dispersion where a dispersive displacement reaches the edge of the grid or an inactive or dry cell (with either method, even where the dispersion is uniform), and, with `"method": "Gsde"`, where the dispersion changes near a face with no wet cell beyond or across a layer face, and can change in layers whose elevation changes from cell to cell. Runs without dispersion are unchanged, to the last bit. Example 5a with dispersion is unchanged within its statistics (`DormandPrince`: GSDE at a 1,000-day `max_dt` to the particle, GSDE and Itô at 250 days within 8 particles of 144,000).

### Fixed
- **Dispersion no longer loses particles through the sides of a model** (`"Gsde"` and `"Ito"`).
  - **What was wrong:** a dispersive displacement whose end lay outside the grid ended the particle as `ExitedDomain`, with an empty termination reason. The sides of a MODFLOW grid and inactive cells are no-flow, so no solute should leave through them by dispersion.
    - Itô lost particles at every such side: in a test row 1 wide with αTH 0.01 and steps of 0.5, 98% were gone after 50 days.
    - The GSDE lost them where the dispersion varies near the side: in the User Guide's Example 2b, a cross-section one row 1 ft wide, 99 of 200 particles left through the side walls within the run.
  - **Now:** the horizontal part of a displacement is followed across the cells' footprints, in the layer the vertical part ended in, and reflected at an edge with no wet cell beyond: the edge of the grid, or an inactive or dry cell. That is the rule for the top and bottom faces since 0.1.7. Solute leaves through such an edge only by advection.
  - **Example 2b now:** all 10,000 particles of a run end `CapturedByBoundary`.
- **The GSDE no longer drops displacements near the sides of a model.** A displacement whose first trial point lay outside the grid was dropped, its x and z parts too. In a one-row model that was about 0.8 σ_y / (row width) of all displacements, and it cut the longitudinal spread by as much: 8% in a test with αTH 0.01 and steps of 0.5.
- **GSDE trial points are reached the way the displacement is:** vertically through the wet cells, then horizontally across the footprints, reflected at faces and edges with no wet cell beyond. A trial point beyond such a face or edge is therefore taken at its mirror image.
  - **At the top and bottom:** up to 0.1.7 a trial point beyond a face with no wet cell beyond was evaluated on the face. Where the dispersion changes with depth, that gathered particles at the face: with D doubling from the bottom to the top of a layer 10 thick (αTV 1), the bottom tenth of an evenly mixed column held 1.9%, 5.4% and 12.3% too much at steps of 0.25, 1 and 5 (review of 0.1.7). It is now within about 2% at steps up to 1. At a step of 5 both faces hold about 5% too little, which is the GSDE's own error where D changes over a step (see Known issues).
  - **At a layer face where the dispersion jumps:** with D four times higher in the upper of two evenly mixed layers, the share in that layer (exactly 0.5) was 0.493, 0.441 and 0.385 at steps of 0.5, 2 and 5. It is now 0.495, 0.500 and 0.503.

### Changed
- **GSDE trial points that move horizontally keep the particle's local z,** like the displacement itself. Up to 0.1.7 they were located by elevation, as in mod-PATH3DU, so in dipping layers they could fall in another layer. In the review's test of dipping layers the result did not depend on the slope, within the noise, in either version.
- **Itô near an edge that flow leaves:** a dispersive displacement across the edge of the grid is now reflected, and the particle leaves with the flow, where it used to end at once. Near an outflow edge of a water-level-map (SSP&A) model the share of particles that has left therefore falls. In a test 200 m upstream of the edge, at a `max_dt` of 5 days, it fell from 0.289 to 0.185 at 350 days, against 0.183 from a finite-volume solution with the usual outflow condition; at a `max_dt` of 50 days, from 0.250 to 0.195. The GSDE's result in that test hardly changed (0.182 to 0.185 at 5 days).
- **Run time** with dispersion was about 8% longer in Example 5a (GSDE), for the walk across the cells.
- **Rust API:** `VelocityField::footprint` gives a cell's footprint polygon; the Waterloo and SSP&A fields provide it. A field without footprints keeps the 0.1.7 horizontal behaviour, now with the termination reason "A dispersive displacement left the grid".

### Known issues
- **Reflection is specular,** a mirror image in the edge or face. That is exact where the flow runs along the wall, as at a no-flow wall. Where the interpolated flow meets a reflecting edge obliquely, such as at the edge of a water-level map that flow leaves, positions along the edge come out up to about one longitudinal dispersivity off.
- **The GSDE needs steps whose dispersive displacement is small against the distance over which D changes:** the layer thickness, the distance to a face where D changes, or the cell size where velocities vary between cells. With D quadrupling over a layer 10 thick (αTV 1), an evenly mixed column came out 9.5% low at the bottom face with steps of 1, and with steps of 5 17% low at the bottom face and 30% high in places inside.
- **Itô cannot represent a jump in D, or in its principal directions, between cells.** At a layer face where the velocity jumps it gathers particles on the side of lower dispersion: in the upper, more dispersive layer of the 4:1 pair above, 0.243, 0.286 and 0.347 instead of 0.5 at steps of 0.5, 2 and 5. In dipping layers its difference probes, taken at fixed elevations, can also move particles out of the model. Use the GSDE.
- **A jump in porosity between layers biases dispersion (since 0.1.5), more with longer steps.** With porosity four times higher in one of two layers, the share in that layer (exactly 0.80) is 0.772 at steps of 0.02 to 0.1, 0.754 at 0.5, 0.722 at 2 and 0.665 at 5. The porosity factors of the GSDE give the right drift where porosity varies smoothly but not at a jump: in their reply to a comment (LaBolle and Zhang 2006, Water Resources Research 42, W02602, doi:10.1029/2005WR004403), the GSDE's authors state that it does not apply where porosity is discontinuous.
- **Where the layer thickness changes from cell to cell, the GSDE takes the capacity as the porosity, not porosity times thickness,** so the drift D·∇b/b (b the saturated thickness) is missing. At a step in thickness under a constant discharge, an evenly mixed solute is depleted on the thicker side next to the step when the flow runs from thick to thin, to about 0.7–0.9 within about αL of the step, and over-filled by about 1.3 to 1.5 times on the thinner side when the flow runs the other way. Itô did not show it in the tests, probably because it cannot see such a jump at all. mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 has the same.
- **Capture by wells depends on the step size.** Capture is checked where each advective step ends, not along the dispersive displacement, so near a well, where the displacement is about as large as the distance to the capture circle, the timing of capture depends on the step. In Example 5a the fraction still in the aquifer at 23,363 days differs between Itô and GSDE by up to 0.003: over two independent sets of 144,000 particles the gap (GSDE above Itô) averaged 0.0013, 0.0022 and 0.0004 at step limits of 1000, 250 and 62.5 days (standard error about 0.0006), about as much as either method's dependence on the step. MP3DU-RS 0.1.6 has the same gap at 250 days. In a test of converging flow to one well with a known solution, both methods approach it as the step shrinks, slowly: at steps of about a day both were still about 0.007 above it.
- **A dispersive displacement is not checked for capture in the cells it passes through.** A displacement that passes through several cells is not captured by a well, or by a boundary on a face, in a cell it only passes through; the advective steps after it are checked where each ends.
- **Near an edge or face that flow leaves, the share that has left depends on the step,** now that dispersion is reflected there and the particle leaves with the flow. In a test of uniform flow toward an outflow edge 2 away (Peclet number 2, GSDE), with steps equal to the travel time to the edge, the share left came out 0.043 above a finite-volume solution at 0.6 of the travel time, 0.013 above at the travel time and 0.011 below at 2.5 times it (0.1.7: 0.037 below, 0.036 above and 0.011 above); with a quarter of that step, 0.022 above at 0.6 of the travel time (0.1.7: 0.007 below).
- **The User Guide's Example 2b against MODFLOW 6 transport:** with 10,000 particles, MP3DU-RS lets about 1 percentage point less of the mass leave through the shallow system (0.298 against 0.309 at day 300), and holds about 1 point more in the low-permeability layers from day 150 to 500, than a MODFLOW 6 transport run with the full dispersion tensor (XT3D) refined to 0.25-ft cells, where it no longer changes with the grid. That is about 2 to 3 standard errors, and it is set near the source, in the split between the shallow and the deep paths. It does not change with steps four times shorter (which do halve a difference of about 2 points in the low-permeability layers at day 50), with the particles run on the finer flow model, or with exits counted where particles enter the constant-head layer. A port of the same particle scheme into mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 gives 0.302, so the two particle codes agree within their noise; the cause, in the particle scheme or in how the two models represent the transport, is not yet known. 0.1.7 shows the same on a row 50 ft wide, where the walls are never reached.
- **The walk across cells assumes convex footprints, shared by the cells above and below.** It has been tested with cells of 1 and 100 at coordinates of about 5 million (as with UTM), and with cells down to a millimetre at coordinates up to 1,000; smaller cells are not supported.
- **Results with dispersion depend on the step size,** and `Euler` with long steps can carry particles through a face where the vertical flow falls to zero, as described for 0.1.7. See "Step Size" in the docs' Dispersion Theory page.
- **Next to a face through which water enters, and beyond which there is no wet cell, dispersion leaves a thin strip empty,** as described for 0.1.7.
- **`DormandPrince` and `CashKarp` can be off by several percent on Waterloo fields,** as described for 0.1.7.
- **Differences from mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9** are as described for 0.1.7. In addition, 2.1.9 drops a GSDE displacement whose first trial point lies outside the grid, and ends a particle whose displacement leaves it; MP3DU-RS now reflects both.

## 0.1.7 (2026-11-25)

Changes since 0.1.6. Results change.
- **Every solver:** results change where a particle crosses the top or bottom face of a cell, or leaves the model through one. The change is large in layered models whose layers have different velocities.
- **With dispersion,** results change in every model. The dispersive displacement of each step now comes first, passes through every layer it reaches and is reflected at no-flow faces, instead of losing the particle, and the particle then moves by advection from where it lands. In the one-layer Example 5a the fraction still in the aquifer at 23,363 days went from 0.950 to 0.940 at mod-PATH3DU's settings, so 0.1.7 no longer agrees with mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 there (see below).
- **`DormandPrince`, `VernerRobust` and `VernerEfficient`:** results also change with dispersion and, without it, by millimetres in layered models (by metres with `relocate_stages: false`).
- **Particles that leave through the top or bottom of the model** now stop on the face, and a boundary there captures them. Scripts that count `ExitedDomain` particles should be checked (see below).
- **Boundaries at IFACE 7** now capture particles as documented. Up to 0.1.6 their capture was reversed: a sink let forward particles through. Results change wherever `bc_iface` contains 7.
- **Unchanged:** runs without dispersion with `Rk4StepDoubling`, `CashKarp` or `Euler`, where no particle comes within a step of the top or bottom face of a cell and no boundary has IFACE 7.

### Fixed
- **A step no longer runs past the top or bottom face of a cell.**
  - **What was wrong:** the stages of a step stay in the starting cell's layer. A step that crossed a top or bottom face therefore moved at that layer's velocity to its end, and the error estimate could not see it, because every stage lay in the same layer. Where the next layer's velocity differs, the particle went too far.
    - **Example:** the mod-PATH3DU User Guide's Example 2, a cross-section with a hydraulic conductivity of 20 ft/d above 1 ft/d, with one particle tracked backward to the water table, at a tolerance of 1e-6 and a 10-day `max_dt`. `Rk4StepDoubling` crossed into the slower layers in one 8-day step. Between x = 20 and 25 ft, where mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9's particle descends through the K = 1 layers in 121 days, 0.1.6's took 6.4 days.
    - **Travel times:** 0.1.6 gave 653.5–726.4 days with the five adaptive solvers, against 635.84 in mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 (step-doubling RK, its default).
  - **Now, with mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9's rules** (from its step-doubling RK, here for every adaptive solver; `Euler`, like 2.1.9's Euler, has only the cut):
    - A trial step that ends more than 1% of the cell's thickness beyond its top or bottom face is rejected.
    - An accepted step that ends beyond the face is cut there: the particle stops on the face, the time advances by the part of the step that took it there, and the particle continues in the cell beyond.
    - These rejections do not count against `max_rejects`. Where 2.1.9 shrinks the step by about 12% per rejection, the retry here is aimed 0.5% of the thickness beyond the face, so one or two retries usually suffice.
    - In the cell beyond, the next step starts again from `initial_dt`, as in 2.1.9. A boundary that captures a particle as it enters a cell (IFACE 0 without a capture radius, 2 or 7) therefore stops a particle that enters through the top or bottom face within one initial step of the face, not up to `max_dt` inside the cell.
  - **Example 2 now:**
    - **Backward:** 635.86 days with `Rk4StepDoubling`, 635.92–636.08 with `CashKarp` and the Verner solvers, and 637.68 with `DormandPrince`. `DormandPrince` reaches 635.84 at a tolerance of 1e-8.
    - **Forward from the end point:** `Rk4StepDoubling`, `CashKarp`, `VernerRobust` and `VernerEfficient` pass the backward release point after 635.82–635.84 days, within 0.0014 ft of it. `DormandPrince` passes after 636.89 days, 0.03 ft away. With 0.1.6, `Rk4StepDoubling` passed after 633.18 days, 0.06 ft away, and `DormandPrince` after 643.31 days, 0.30 ft away.
  - **The User Guide's Example 6** (MODPATH 7's Example 2: MODFLOW-USG, three layers with a confining unit, 16 particles tracked backward from a well to the water table), with `Rk4StepDoubling`: 0.1.6 differed from mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 by up to 0.93% in travel time and 29 ft in the end points, and now by up to 0.023% and 1.1 ft.
  - **The User Guide's Example 7** (a MODFLOW 6 island, with the sea as a general-head boundary at IFACE 7): the particles are now captured within 0.21 days and 0.02 m of where 2.1.9 captures them, on entering the sea cells through their bottom face. Without the restart from `initial_dt` they went one 10-day step further.
  - **Exits through the top or bottom of the model:** a particle that reaches such a face now stops on it, and that point is recorded with the time at which the particle reaches it. The same applies at a face with a dry cell beyond.
    - **Before:** the step that crossed the face was dropped, so the last record and the final state were those of the step before.
    - **Termination reason:** "No wet cell beyond the top face" (or bottom face). Before, it was empty.
    - **Boundaries on that face** (IFACE 6 or 5) now capture the particle (`CapturedByBoundary`, "Top face sink/source: …"): a sink there, such as a drain, captures particles tracked forward, and a source, such as recharge or the constant heads on the top face in Example 2, captures particles tracked backward. Before, the crossing step ended it as `ExitedDomain`, unless a step happened to end within `face_epsilon` of the face.
    - **Capture order, as in mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9:** a particle that reaches a face is recorded on it, and checked for capture, in the cell it is leaving. Only then does it move into the cell beyond, where it starts on that cell's face.
  - **A face where the vertical flow converges from both sides,** such as a boundary flow assigned to an interior face, or flows that do not balance, holds a particle that reaches it. It now ends as `Stagnated`, "Stuck on a layer face: …", at the time it reaches the face. Without this check, the particle would have crossed the face back and forth, without the time advancing, until `max_steps`.
  - **With dispersion,** the dispersive displacement is not cut at a face, because that would shrink its random part; the advective steps that follow it are cut as above (see the next item).
- **Dispersion moves particles correctly between layers** (`"method": "Gsde"` and `"Ito"`).
  - **What was wrong:**
    - **Steps spanning more than one layer:** such a step went to the next cell only, and its local z was clamped there, so it stopped on that cell's far face. Thin layers and long steps therefore cut the vertical spread. In 80 identical layers, with steps spanning 6 layers, the vertical variance came out at 5% of the exact value, and most particles ended on a face.
    - **No-flow top or bottom:** a step that took a particle through the top or bottom of the model, or into a dry cell, ended it as `ExitedDomain`, even where no water leaves, so mass was lost. In a test with a no-flow top and bottom, 16–39% of the particles were lost within 6 days.
    - **Wrong frame:** the elevation of a particle moving to the next cell was computed with the cells' geometric top and bottom, while its local z is measured in the saturated thickness. Where the water table lies below a cell's top, the particle landed at the wrong elevation.
  - **Now, each step with dispersion has two parts:**
    - **The dispersive displacement comes first,** computed where the particle is. Its vertical part is followed through every cell it reaches, in each cell's saturated thickness. At a face with no wet cell beyond (the top or bottom of the model, a dry cell, or the water table) it is reflected: no solute disperses through such a face.
    - **The particle then moves by advection from where it lands,** for the length of the step, exactly as without dispersion: an advective step that reaches a top or bottom face is cut there, and the particle continues in the cell beyond. It leaves through a face with no wet cell beyond only where the flow carries it out, as with the usual zero-gradient (Danckwerts) outflow condition; a boundary there can capture it, and otherwise it ends `ExitedDomain`, "No wet cell beyond the top face" (or bottom face).
    - **The next displacement comes when the step's full length has passed,** however the advective motion was divided at faces. A step here is the interval between two displacements; the advective motion within it can take several solver steps. Its length follows the solver's error control as if there were no faces: where the error is too small to matter it grows fourfold per step (or by `max_scale`, if smaller), up to `max_dt`.
    - **Layers:** where the flow is uniform, how a column is divided into layers does not change the result: in the column test below, 1 layer of 10, 20 of 0.5, or 0.1 over 9.9 give identical results, with fixed steps and with steps that grow from `initial_dt` 0.1 to `max_dt` 2. Where the velocity varies within the cells, 1, 10 and 100 layers leave the same solute with fixed steps.
    - **But with steps that may grow,** the steps in thin layers, where most advective steps are cut at a face, stay near `initial_dt`, while in one thick layer they follow the solver's error control, and the results differ as results with those step sizes do. In a test with the velocity rising by 10% per unit height and `initial_dt` 0.1, 0.126 of the solute was left in 100 layers and 0.150 in one layer; fixed steps of 0.1 gave 0.123, and a finite-volume solution 0.109 (the rest is the GSDE's error next to the faces, see the known issues). Keep `initial_dt` no larger than a step you would accept for the dispersion.
    - **A face where the vertical flow converges from both sides** stops a dispersing particle as it does one without dispersion: it ends `Stagnated`, "Stuck on a layer face". Such a face carries a sink, which captures the particle first, or flows that do not balance.
  - **Against a finite-difference solution** for a column with upward flow (Danckwerts conditions), with steps of a twentieth of the transit time, the solute left after one transit time is 1.4% too low at a Peclet number of 10 and 0.4% too low at 1; with smaller steps the difference vanishes.
  - **Example 5a** (the MEUK model with three wells, 144,000 particles), fraction still in the aquifer at 23,363 and 50,000 days:
    - **0.1.7 no longer agrees with mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 here.** With mod-PATH3DU's own settings (step-doubling Runge-Kutta, no step limit), MP3DU-RS 0.1.7 gives 0.940 and 0.029, and mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 0.950 and 0.025, as MP3DU-RS 0.1.5 and 0.1.6 did (0.950 and 0.026). The difference, about 14 standard errors at 23,363 days, comes from the order of the two parts of a step: 2.1.9 takes the advection from where the step starts.
    - **Dormand-Prince:** 0.940 and 0.0295 with a 1,000-day `max_dt`, and 0.942 and 0.029 with 250 and with 62.5 days. With the order of 0.1.6 (and the fix of the reused first stage below), 0.953, 0.947 and 0.943 at 23,363 days, still moving.
    - **MT3D** gives 0.944 and 0.029. At 23,363 days 0.1.7 is about as far below it as 2.1.9 is above it (0.004 and 0.006); at 50,000 days, and with smaller steps, 0.1.7 is closer. MT3D has errors of its own, and neither particle code has been shown to converge on this model.
    - The result of 0.1.7 hardly depends on the step size here.
  - **Tests:**
    - Identical layers give the same particle positions as one layer of the same total thickness (the test allows differences of 1e-9): 80 layers without vertical flow, with the vertical variance within 15% of 2·αTV·v·t; 8 layers with slow vertical flow; and, with strong upward flow, 20 layers of 0.5, or 0.1 over 9.9, with fixed and with growing steps.
    - Where the vertical flow converges on a face, no particle leaves through the top or bottom; those that reach the face end there, `Stagnated`.
    - No particle is lost at no-flow boundaries: with either dispersion method where there is no vertical flow, and with the GSDE where the vertical velocity falls to zero at the face, with every solver and tracked backward.
    - Where the flow leaves through a face, particles leave through it, and through no other.
    - A drain on the top face (forward) or recharge there (backward) captures every particle that the flow carries to it.
    - The solute left in a column with upward flow matches the finite-difference solution at 5, 10 and 15 days and Peclet numbers 10 and 1, within 4 standard errors (100,000 particles).
- **The Itô method no longer throws a particle that lies on a face between layers.** A particle exactly on a top or bottom face, as after a step cut there or a release on a face, had the gradient of the dispersion taken across the face; where the velocity, and so the dispersion, differs between the layers, the drift came out about a million times too large. In a test with velocities of 1 and 0.5 in two layers, particles released on the face between them ended at a mean x of 654 after 5 days, against 4. Such a particle is now taken just inside its cell (2e-6 of the thickness) for the dispersion.
- **Boundaries at IFACE 7 capture as documented.**
  - **What was wrong:** `hydrate_cell_flows()` reversed the sign of the flow of every `bc_iface` 7 entry. mod-PATH3DU does that only for flows to MODFLOW-USG CLN cells; for ordinary cells it keeps MODFLOW's sign (`cls_flowmodel.cpp`). An IFACE 7 sink, such as a general-head boundary taking water out of the aquifer, therefore did not capture forward particles, which passed through its cell, and an IFACE 7 source captured them. Backward tracking was reversed the same way.
  - **Example:** in the User Guide's Example 7 (a MODFLOW 6 island, with the sea as a general-head boundary at IFACE 7), the particles of 0.1.6 passed through the sea cells and left the model through their top after 5,126–5,757 days. mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 stops them as they enter the sea cells, after 3,477–3,867 days, and so does MODFLOW 6 PRT.
  - **Now:** IFACE 7 entries capture like IFACE 2: a sink as a forward particle enters its cell, a source as a backward particle does. Velocities were not affected, because the flow terms (`q_top` and the others) are given separately. Scripts that changed the sign of IFACE 7 flows to work around this must stop doing so.
- **`DormandPrince`, `VernerRobust` and `VernerEfficient` evaluate the first stage of every step at the particle.**
  - **What was wrong:** they took it from the last stage of the previous step ("first same as last"), which is exact only if the particle starts the step where that stage was evaluated, in the same cell. Two things broke that:
    - **Dispersion:** the dispersive displacement moved the particle after the advective step. The stale velocity inflated the error estimate, so the steps near wells shrank.
      - **Example 5a** (the MEUK model, Dormand-Prince with a 1,000-day step limit), with this change alone: 0.1.6 took 55.9 steps per particle to 50,000 days, and 43.9 with the change.
      - **Particles still in the aquifer** (144,000 particles), at 23,363 and 50,000 days, with this change alone: from 0.948 and 0.0265 to 0.953 and 0.0249. The change to dispersion above changes them again (see there).
    - **A move into another cell:** the reused velocity belonged to the previous cell's layer, and with `relocate_stages: false` to the previous cell's velocity field. In a test without dispersion, on a two-layer grid with small velocity jumps at the cell faces:
      - with `relocate_stages: true` (the default), end points moved by up to 4 mm at a tolerance of 1e-6 (9 mm at 1e-8, with `euler_dt` 1e-2), and those of particles that stayed in one layer by less than 1e-7 m;
      - with `relocate_stages: false`, end points moved by up to 68 m.
  - **Cost:** a step that follows an accepted step takes one more velocity evaluation, 7 instead of 6 for Dormand-Prince and 9 instead of 8 for the Verner solvers. A particle's first step, and retries of rejected steps, cost the same as before.
  - **The earlier explanation was wrong:** the notes for 0.1.5 and 0.1.6 put the Dormand-Prince difference in Example 5a down to MP3DU-RS letting the step grow fivefold and mod-PATH3DU fourfold. Both cap the growth at fourfold.
  - **That difference was measured against mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9's Dormand-Prince option,** which is incomplete and not meant for use, so it is not a reference. With step-doubling Runge-Kutta, mod-PATH3DU's default integrator, the two programs agree, as before.

### Known issues
- **A jump in porosity between layers biases dispersion (since 0.1.5).** At such a jump, the GSDE does not keep an evenly mixed solute evenly mixed: layers of lower porosity collect too many particles.
  - **Size:** in a test across one interface, with porosity four times higher below and the same dispersion on both sides, 77.8% of the particles ended below, against an exact 80%. Smaller steps do not change that.
  - **Likely cause:** the porosity factor of the GSDE step gives the right drift where porosity varies smoothly, but not at a jump.
  - **Not affected:** jumps in dispersivity or velocity alone are handled. A porosity ratio of 2 gave 0.660 against an exact 0.667 below, within 2 standard errors.
- **A dispersive displacement is not checked for capture in the cells it passes through.** A displacement that passes through several cells is not captured by a well, or by a boundary on a face, in a cell it only passes through; the advective steps after it are checked where each ends.
- **Next to a face through which water enters, and beyond which there is no wet cell, dispersion leaves a thin strip empty.** Examples are the bottom of a model with upward flow, or a water table with recharge. The dispersive part of a step is reflected at such a face, and the advective part then moves the particle away from it, so no particle ends a step closer to the face than that step's advective displacement. In the column test above, after 15 days the strip as thick as one step's advection held no particles, where the exact solution has 3.6% of the solute then in the column (Peclet number 1). The strip narrows with the step. In 0.1.6 a particle that dispersed through such a face was lost.
- **`DormandPrince` and `CashKarp` can be off by several percent on Waterloo fields** at the usual tolerance of 1e-6, probably because their error estimates do not see the velocity jumps between cells well. In the User Guide's Example 2, `DormandPrince` gives 637.68 days, 0.3% long (635.84 at a tolerance of 1e-8). In Example 3 (the quad-patch grid of Pollock 2015, Fig. 4b), against MODFLOW 6 PRT, both are 2–7% off on the short paths beside the constant-head block, where `Rk4StepDoubling` and the Verner solvers are close. The User Guide's examples use `Rk4StepDoubling`.
- **Differences from mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 that remain:**
  - **With dispersion,** 2.1.9 takes the advection of a step from where the step starts, and MP3DU-RS from where the displacement lands, so their results differ (Example 5a above). 2.1.9 also skips dispersion in cells with a well and tightens the step error a hundredfold there; MP3DU-RS does neither. Retardation is not applied (see Changed).
  - A particle tracked forward that reaches a water table with no boundary on it ends `ExitedDomain` ("No wet cell beyond the top face"); 2.1.9 ends it captured ("Water_Table (No BC present)"). Counts of statuses therefore differ.
  - With dispersion, 2.1.9 reflects a particle at every face with no wet cell beyond and stops its vertical advection there for the rest of the step, so its particles hardly ever leave through such a face or reach a boundary on it. MP3DU-RS lets the advective part of the step carry them out.
- **Results with dispersion depend on the step size.** See "Step Size" in the docs' Dispersion Theory page. In Example 5a the dependence is now small (0.940 still in the aquifer at 23,363 days with a 1,000-day `max_dt`, 0.942 with 250 and 62.5 days), but in the column test long steps leave far too little solute (in a review run with `initial_dt` 0.1 and `max_dt` 1000, a hundred transit times, 22% too little at a Peclet number of 1). `Euler` with steps longer than about 1/(dv/dz) carries particles through a face where the vertical flow falls to zero, with or without dispersion. Check results at a smaller `max_dt`.
- **The GSDE near a top or bottom face where the dispersion changes with depth.** A trial point of the GSDE beyond such a face is evaluated at the face. Where the dispersion coefficient changes with depth, an evenly mixed solute then does not stay evenly mixed next to the face: in a test with the vertical dispersion doubling from bottom to top, the density in the bottom tenth of the column was 5% too high with steps of 1 and 12% with steps of 5. In the column test with the velocity rising by 10% per unit height and a Peclet number of 1, the solute left after one transit time was 31% too high with steps of 0.5 and 13% with steps of 0.1 (finite-volume solution 0.109). The Itô method keeps an evenly mixed solute evenly mixed (within 1%), and was within 3.5% in that column test.

### Changed
- **With dispersion, a pathline record's velocity is the advective velocity** of the step that ends there; the dispersive displacement, made at the start of each step, shows in the positions only. Up to 0.1.6 the velocity included the dispersive part. The capture checks on top and bottom faces, the stagnation test and the drift-capture test likewise use the advective velocity. An advective step cut at a face gives a record of its own, so runs with dispersion give somewhat more records in layered models.
- **`"retardation_enabled": true` gives a `UserWarning`.** MP3DU-RS does not apply retardation, so such a run gives the results of an unretarded solute. Up to 0.1.6 the setting was accepted without a word, which made it easy to publish unretarded results by mistake. The warning comes from `SimulationConfig.from_json()` and `.validate()`, like the warning about `euler_dt`.
- **`Rk4StepDoubling` no longer evaluates the velocity at the start of a step repeatedly.** The full step and the first half step start at the same point, and each evaluated the velocity there, again on every retry of a rejected step. That velocity is now kept once evaluated, so a trial step that runs to the end takes 11 velocity evaluations instead of 12 the first time, and 10 instead of 12 on each retry. On its own, this change leaves the results identical, bit for bit.

## 0.1.6 (2026-11-23)

Changes since 0.1.5. Scripts that run with dispersion and pass dispersivity arrays to `hydrate_cell_properties()` stop with an error and must be changed (see below); with the arrays removed they give the same results as 0.1.5. MEUK (SSP&A) scripts with a vertical transverse dispersivity other than 0 also stop with an error. Results without dispersion are unchanged.

### Changed
- **Dispersivities are given per cell or in the configuration, and per-cell values are used.** `hydrate_cell_properties()` required the arrays `disp_long`, `disp_trans_h` and `disp_trans_v`, but the tracker never read them: every cell had the configuration's `alpha_l`, `alpha_th` and `alpha_tv`. A model whose dispersivities differ between layers or zones was therefore tracked with one set everywhere, without notice. mod-PATH3DU takes them per cell for MODFLOW models, from the three dispersivity arrays of its `.p3d` file (labelled `DISPH`, `DISPT` and `DISPV` in the Example 5a input). The dispersivities are now given in one of two ways:
  - per cell, as the three arrays, which are now optional, to `hydrate_cell_properties()` or, new in this version, to `hydrate_sspa_inputs()` (mod-PATH3DU has no per-cell dispersivities for MEUK models), with only `"method"` in the configuration's `dispersion` block;
  - as the three numbers in the `dispersion` block, the same in every cell, with the arrays left out.

  With per-cell values the particle uses its own cell's dispersivities, and each trial point of the GSDE uses those of the cell it lies in, as in mod-PATH3DU's algorithm; results with dispersivity zones have not yet been compared with mod-PATH3DU's. A constant given either way gives the same trajectories, to the last bit. With dispersion off, per-cell arrays are not used, and `run_simulation()` issues a `UserWarning` saying so unless every value is 0; scripts for 0.1.5 that pass arrays of zeros without dispersion therefore run as before, without a warning.
- **A run with dispersion refuses dispersivities given both ways, or neither.** `run_simulation()` raises `ValueError` before it tracks any particle, with "dispersivity is given twice … Use one of them …" or "dispersion is on but no dispersivity is given …". Scripts written for earlier versions that run with dispersion give both, because the arrays were required (often as zeros), and now stop with the first message. Removing the arrays keeps the results of 0.1.5, which used the configuration's values: GSDE, Itô and SSP&A test runs give trajectories identical to those of 0.1.5. Giving only one or two of the three arrays, or of the three numbers, is also an error, as is a negative dispersivity, or a NaN or infinite value in the arrays. Every cell is checked, inactive ones included.
- **Two more checks on dispersivities.**
  - The Itô method refuses per-cell dispersivities that differ between cells. It takes its drift from differences of the dispersion tensor, which cannot represent a change from one cell to the next; use the GSDE for such dispersivities, or give one set in the configuration and leave out the arrays. The values must be identical, inactive cells included.
  - An SSP&A field, which is two-dimensional, refuses a vertical transverse dispersivity other than 0. Before, such a value moved particles out through the top or bottom of the model, where they ended as `ExitedDomain`. mod-PATH3DU sets it to 0 for MEUK models.
- `CellProperties.disp_long`, `disp_trans_h` and `disp_trans_v` are `None` when the arrays are not given, and `SspaInputs` has the same three properties. In `mp3du_schema.json`, `alpha_l`, `alpha_th` and `alpha_tv` are optional, all three or none.
- Rust:
  - `GsdeConfig` and `ItoConfig` hold `Option<f64>` dispersivities, and `uniform()` builds one with all three set.
  - `DispersionMethodConfig::configured_dispersivities()` replaces `dispersivities()`.
  - `run_simulation()` and `run_simulation_parallel()` take the per-cell dispersivities as a new last argument, and `track_single_particle()` takes the run's `Dispersivity`, as `Dispersivity::resolve()` decides it.
  - `CellProperties` holds `dispersivity: Option<Vec<[f64; 3]>>` in place of the three vectors.
  - `VelocityField::is_two_dimensional()` is true for the SSP&A field.

### Documentation
- The README, the schema and the reference pages described retardation as applied (`retardation_enabled` dividing velocities by the retardation factor). MP3DU-RS has never applied retardation; they now say that it has no effect.

### Known differences from mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9
- mod-PATH3DU skips dispersion in cells that contain a well and tightens the step error a hundredfold there; MP3DU-RS does neither.
- `retardation_enabled` has no effect, because MP3DU-RS does not apply retardation. mod-PATH3DU's `RETARDATION` option multiplies each cell's porosity by its retardation factor, which slows both advection and dispersion.
- With Dormand-Prince and a 1,000-day step limit, Example 5a gives 0.948 and 0.0265 in MP3DU-RS 0.1.5 against 0.952 and 0.0252 in mod-PATH3DU with the same settings, a difference of 2 to 4 standard errors. The two Dormand-Prince step controllers differ in detail (MP3DU-RS lets the step grow fivefold, mod-PATH3DU fourfold); with step-doubling Runge-Kutta the results agree.

## 0.1.5 (2026-11-23)

Changes since 0.1.4. Results with dispersion change; results without dispersion are unchanged.

### Fixed
- **The GSDE dispersion method produces the drift term.** A random walk reproduces the advection-dispersion equation only with the drift θ⁻¹∇·(θD) added to the random step, and the GSDE of LaBolle et al. (2000) obtains it by evaluating the dispersion at trial points with the same random numbers. MP3DU-RS drew the second step from independent random numbers, which removes the drift, so particles collected where dispersion is low; its two-step form (eq. 11a) is also not exact for anisotropic dispersion. The method now follows LaBolle et al. (2000, eqs. 17a–17c), as mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 has since November 2026: one random vector per step, trial points located by position, including in the layer above or below, and the ratio of porosities between trial point and particle. In a strip with uniform flow and transverse dispersivity 1.0 on one side and 0.1 on the other, an evenly spread set of particles now stays even to within 2%; before, the low-dispersion half held 1.73 times its share. In Example 5a (the MEUK model, 144,000 particles), run with the integrator settings of the mod-PATH3DU input (step-doubling Runge-Kutta, no step limit), the fraction of particles still in the aquifer at 23,363 and 50,000 days is 0.950 and 0.0259, against 0.950 and 0.0247 to 0.0254 in four runs of mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9, which is within the Monte Carlo noise, and 0.944 and 0.029 for MT3D.
- **Backward tracking keeps the sign of the dispersive drift.** The tracker multiplied the dispersive displacement by the signed time step, which reversed the drift in backward tracking; the backward process has the drift of the forward one. This affected both dispersion methods.
- **Vertical dispersion is in the right units, in the right layer.** The dispersion methods were given the cell's local z (0 to 1) as if it were an elevation, and their vertical result, a length per time, was used as local z per time, so vertical dispersion was scaled by the cell thickness. Trial points were also located by footprint alone, so in a layered model they could fall in another layer. Positions and vertical velocities are now global within the dispersion methods, and trial points are found in the particle's layer and then above or below, passing through dry cells as mod-PATH3DU does.

### Changed
- **Itô results change slightly.** The Itô method now builds the dispersion tensor from the velocity at the start of the step, as the Itô scheme requires, instead of the step's average velocity. In Example 5a the fraction of particles still in the aquifer at 50,000 days changed from 0.028 to 0.0265.
- Rust: `GsdeDispersion::new` and `ItoDispersion::new` take only the particle id; `DispersionCalculator::calc` takes the particle position in global coordinates, the medium there (`MediumAt`), the cell thickness, the signed time step, and a function giving the medium at a point. `VelocityField::cell_medium` and `CellMedium` give a cell's vertical frame and porosity, and `DispersionMethodConfig::dispersivities` the dispersivities.

### Known differences from mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9
- mod-PATH3DU skips dispersion in cells that contain a well and tightens the step error a hundredfold there; MP3DU-RS does neither.
- `retardation_enabled` has no effect: velocities are not divided by the retardation factor. The dispersivities given to `hydrate_cell_properties()` are not used; those of the configuration are.
- With Dormand-Prince and a 1,000-day step limit, Example 5a gives 0.948 and 0.0265 in MP3DU-RS against 0.952 and 0.0252 in mod-PATH3DU with the same settings, a difference of 2 to 4 standard errors. The two Dormand-Prince step controllers differ in detail (MP3DU-RS lets the step grow fivefold, mod-PATH3DU fourfold); with step-doubling Runge-Kutta the results agree.

## 0.1.4 (2026-11-23)

Changes since 0.1.3. Results are unchanged for valid input, except where noted below.

### Fixed
- **A panic in Rust raises a Python exception instead of ending Python.** The release build, from which the wheel is made, aborted on a panic, so any panic in the Rust code ended the Python process without a traceback. The build now unwinds, and PyO3 raises `pyo3_runtime.PanicException`. That exception derives from `BaseException`, not `Exception`, so `except Exception` does not catch it. The input errors known to have caused panics are now reported as `ValueError`, as described below. A Python environment without NumPy now fails at `import mp3du` with `ModuleNotFoundError`; before, the first `hydrate_*` call ended the process.
- **`fit_waterloo()` and `fit_sspa()` check all of their inputs before they take the grid.** A rejected call raises `ValueError` and leaves the `GridHandle` loaded; before, most rejections left it empty, and a `CellFlows` longer than the grid with a well in one of the extra cells ended the process. `fit_waterloo()` now also rejects a `top_neighbor` or `bot_neighbor` id that is not a cell of the grid, which ended the process when a particle crossed that face, and a `nodes_per_layer` that does not divide the number of cells, with which a particle could remain in one cell until `max_steps`. `run_simulation()` rejects a particle whose `cell_id` is not a cell of the field; with the `Rk4StepDoubling` solver such a particle ended the process.
- **`is_domain_boundary` and `has_water_table` take effect without boundary arrays.** `hydrate_cell_flows()` applied the two flags only when the four `bc_*` arrays were also given and ignored them otherwise, so a particle that reached a flagged cell continued and left the grid instead of being captured as "Model Boundary". The flags now apply in both cases. Scripts that passed `is_domain_boundary` without boundary arrays will see particles captured where they were not before.
- **SSP&A results are reproducible.** With two or more line-drift groups, the order of the drift terms in the kriging system depended on a randomly seeded hash table, and the same input gave velocities that differed in the last digits (about 1e-12 relative) from run to run. The groups are now taken in the order of their first segment, after the wells in list order; results are identical from run to run, but may differ from a given 0.1.3 run in the last digits.
- **The boundary name in `termination_reason` is reproducible.** When two boundary types in one cell could capture a particle, the name reported changed from run to run; the type with the lower `bc_type_id` is now reported.

### Changed
- **Unknown keys in the simulation configuration are an error.** `SimulationConfig.from_json()` dropped keys it did not recognize, so a misspelled key was ignored without notice: `"capture_raduis": 0.5` left `capture_radius` unset, which is capture on entry to a well cell. Such a key now raises `ValueError` naming the key and the keys accepted. The configurations in the documentation use only known keys; a script whose configuration carries extra keys, such as a `_comment` entry, must remove them. Extra keys beside `"method": "None"` in `dispersion` are still ignored.
- **The wheel declares its requirements and carries type information.** The wheel's metadata now lists NumPy as a dependency and Python 3.8 as the minimum, so installing the wheel installs NumPy where it is missing; before, it declared neither. The type stub is now part of the wheel (`mp3du/__init__.pyi`, with `py.typed`), so editors and type checkers show the signatures and docstrings; before, it was kept in the source repository only. The stub is also corrected: it lacked the `top_neighbor`, `bot_neighbor` and `nodes_per_layer` arguments of `fit_waterloo()`, the `hydraulic_conductivity` argument of `hydrate_sspa_inputs()`, `VelocityFieldHandle.velocity_at()` and the properties of `SspaInputs`, and gave `SspaConfig` a required `search_radius`. A test now compares the stub with the compiled module. The Python tests pass on Python 3.8 with NumPy 1.24 and on Python 3.14 with NumPy 2.5.
- **The wheel names SSP&A's licence.** The wheel's metadata declared the MIT licence, which the Cargo manifests carried, although MP3DU-RS is distributed under SSP&A's end-user licence agreement (`license.txt`). The metadata now reads `License-Expression: LicenseRef-SSPA-EULA`, the agreement itself is in the wheel (`.dist-info/licenses/license.txt`), and the Cargo manifests refer to it with `license-file`.
- **Integer arrays may have any integer dtype.** `face_offset` had to be `uint64`, and `bc_iface` and `bc_type_id` had to be `int32`, so arrays made with NumPy's default integer type (`int64`) were rejected. `face_offset`, `face_neighbor`, `bc_cell_ids`, `bc_iface` and `bc_type_id` now accept a 1-D array of any integer dtype; each value is checked against the range of the type used inside, and a value that does not fit, such as a negative offset, raises `ValueError` naming the array, the value and its index. Values are never cast silently. Float arrays must still be `float64` and masks `bool`.
- **A wrong array dtype is reported in plain terms.** An array of the wrong dtype or shape, or a list where an array is expected, raised `TypeError: argument 'head': 'ndarray' object cannot be converted to 'PyArray<T, D>'`. The message now names the dtype and dimensions expected and those given, for example `expected a 1-D NumPy array of dtype float64, got a 1-D array of dtype int64`, and the conversion that fixes it.
- **`mp3du_schema.json` accepts what `to_json()` writes.** The schema lacked `adaptive.relocate_stages` and did not allow `capture_radius` to be null, so it rejected the configuration that `to_json()` produces. Both are added. The copy of the schema in the documentation repository is not yet updated.
- Rust: `WaterlooField::check_inputs()` and `SspaField::check_inputs()` run the checks of `new()` without building the field. `WaterlooFieldBuildError` has two new variants, `NeighborOutOfRange` and `NodesPerLayerMismatch`.

## 0.1.2 (2026-11-18)

Changes since 0.1.1.

### Fixed
- **The Waterloo method uses aquifer storage.** `fit_waterloo()` subtracts `cell_flows.q_storage` from the vertical term `q_vert`, in both the fit of each cell and the velocity evaluation, as mod-PATH3DU 2.1.9 does (`qvert = (qtop - qbot) + (qtopface - qbotface) - qsto`). Storage acts as a source or sink spread evenly over the cell. In 0.1.0 and 0.1.1, `hydrate_cell_flows()` accepted `q_storage` and kept it, but nothing used it: passing storage gave the same result as passing zeros. In transient models the horizontal velocity was therefore wrong wherever storage is a sizable part of a cell's flow. In a transient MODFLOW 6 test model with 15-day steps, using storage moved particle end positions by 0.68 m on average and by up to 39 m. In a closed-form case with uniform storage, the path error after 300 days fell from 15.8 m to 0.44 m.

### Changed
- **Pass storage in `q_storage`, as MODFLOW gives it.** `q_storage` is the storage flow in MODFLOW's sign and in volume per time: positive is water released from storage into the cell. For MODFLOW 6 pass `STO-SS + STO-SY` per cell (through the API, `GWF/STO/STRGSS + GWF/STO/STRGSY`); for MODFLOW-2005 and MODFLOW-USG pass `STORAGE`; for steady state pass zeros. Do not negate it.
- **Check scripts written for 0.1.1.** A non-zero `q_storage` passed to 0.1.1 now takes effect. If you added storage into `q_vert` yourself (for example `q_vert = -storage`, or `q_vert` set to the cell residual `sum(face_flow) + q_well + q_other`, which already contains storage), either keep that and pass `q_storage` as zeros, or take storage out of `q_vert` and pass it in `q_storage`. Doing both counts storage twice.
- Steady-state models, and scripts that pass `q_storage` as zeros, give results identical to 0.1.1, bit for bit.
- **Documentation: boundary flows through the top and bottom faces belong in `q_vert`.** The documentation said that flows routed to the top or bottom face (IFACE 5, 6 and 7, such as recharge) must be left out of `q_vert`. mod-PATH3DU includes them, and the Waterloo fit needs them: leaving recharge out of `q_vert` leaves it out of the horizontal velocity, as leaving storage out did. For a cell whose budget closes, `q_vert = sum(face_flow) + q_well + q_other + q_storage` (`face_flow` positive into the cell, the rest in raw MODFLOW sign). The code did not change on this point; scripts that followed the old advice should be checked.

### Added
- Rust: `mp3du_grid::flow::qvert_with_storage(q_vert, q_storage)` gives the vertical term that `CellFitInput::q_vert` and `CellEvalContext::q_vert` expect, for Rust users who build these directly.
- The docstrings of `hydrate_cell_flows()`, `hydrate_waterloo_inputs()` and `fit_waterloo()`, and the type stub, describe `q_vert` and `q_storage`. The stub lists the keys of `TrajectoryResult.to_records()`: `step`, `time`, `x`, `y`, `z`, `vx`, `vy`, `vz`, `cell_id` and `dt`.
- Tests: storage passed in `q_storage` gives the same trajectories as the same storage folded into `q_vert`; and a closed-form case with uniform storage.

## 0.1.1 (2026-11-17)

Changes since 0.1.0.

### Fixed
- **Adaptive solvers no longer stay on the Euler fallback.** In 0.1.0, once the step size dropped to `adaptive.euler_dt` or below, every later step was a first-order Euler step of `euler_dt`, and `tolerance` had no further effect. With `euler_dt` at 1.0 or 0.1, as the 0.1.0 README and examples had it, `DormandPrince`, `CashKarp`, the Verner solvers and `Rk4StepDoubling` all ran as fixed-step Euler. After the one Euler step, the next step now grows by `safety / 0.5^alpha`, as in the C++, and adaptive stepping resumes.
- **A pathline ends on `capture.max_time`.** Every step is limited to the tracking time that is left, as in the C++. In 0.1.0 the last step could overshoot `max_time` by up to `max_dt`.
- **A particle that reaches the edge of the domain ends as `ExitedDomain`.** In 0.1.0 it could end as `SolverFailed` (with `min_dt` at 1e-10 and a smaller `euler_dt`), and `Rk4StepDoubling` could stall there until `max_steps`.
- **`Rk4StepDoubling` reports a dry or outside start cell at once,** like the other solvers. In 0.1.0 it retried up to `max_rejects` times first.
- **`adaptive.min_dt` above `max_dt` is rejected** with a `ValueError` when the configuration is built. In 0.1.0 it was accepted: every particle then ended as `SolverFailed` at its first step, or, with the `Euler` solver, tracking panicked.

### Changed
- **`tolerance` is relative to the model extent.** The step error in each direction is divided by the model extent in that direction (`xmax - xmin`, `ymax - ymin`, `zmax - zmin`), as in the C++. In 0.1.0 it was divided by the particle's own coordinates, so the accuracy depended on where the grid's origin was. Step counts and results change slightly. Models in large coordinates, such as UTM, now take smaller and more accurate steps.
- **The `dt` of a trajectory record is the step taken to reach it** (0 at the release point), as the documentation says. In 0.1.0 it held the suggested next step.
- **Steps rejected because a stage point left the domain no longer count toward `max_rejects`.**

### Added
- `SimulationConfig.from_json()` and `.validate()` issue a Python `UserWarning` when `adaptive.euler_dt` is used like a step size: above `adaptive.min_dt` and not small next to `initial_dt`.

### Guidance
- Set `adaptive.euler_dt` equal to `adaptive.min_dt`, for example both `1e-10`. `euler_dt` is the threshold of the adaptive solvers' emergency Euler step, which makes it a floor on the step size. The `Euler` solver does not use it; it steps at `initial_dt`. This applies to 0.1.0 as well.
